Module 1

Wholesale Banking Fundamentals

Wholesale banking serves large organizations: corporates, governments, financial institutions, insurers, funds, and multinationals. Compared to retail, it involves larger transaction volumes, cross-border complexity, treasury sensitivity, and stronger control obligations.

Retail: individual customer, branch-centric, standardized products.
Wholesale: relationship-led, bespoke solutions, high-value flows.
Expand: Why payments are the bloodstream
  • Supplier continuity and payroll trust.
  • Treasury liquidity positioning.
  • Trade cycle continuity and settlement certainty.
  • Client confidence and service-level credibility.
Presenter note: Emphasize that a single failed high-value payment can carry reputational and liquidity consequences far beyond its face value.
Module 2

Payments Types & Rails

Domestic flows occur within one country. Cross-border flows include additional layers: FX conversion, intermediary routing, sanctions checks, cut-off windows, and time-zone dependencies.

High-value: urgent treasury movements, securities settlement, strategic supplier payouts.
Low-value: recurring and bulk transactions such as utilities, collections, and routine transfers.
Expand: Common rails and methods
  • SWIFT messaging network (instruction transport, not money movement).
  • CHIPS / Fedwire / TARGET2 / SEPA depending on geography and purpose.
  • Wire transfer, ACH-style bulk processing, and real-time rails.
Presenter note: Ask the room which rails they use today and where exceptions usually arise.
Module 3

End-to-End Payment Lifecycle

Lifecycle sequence: Initiation → Validation → Enrichment → Routing → Clearing → Settlement → Reconciliation.

  • Initiation via portal, API, host-to-host file, or branch instruction.
  • Validation checks format, required fields, approvals, funds, duplicates.
  • Enrichment adds routing identifiers, intermediaries, charges, purpose.
  • Routing selects path by currency, urgency, amount, reachability.
  • Clearing exchanges obligations; settlement finalizes value transfer.
  • Reconciliation aligns internal books with external confirmations.
Expand: Operational risk concentration points
Validation and routing carry the highest operational risk concentration because bad data and poor route selection propagate downstream delays and repair cost.
Module 4

SWIFT & ISO 20022

Legacy SWIFT MT messages remain widely used (MT103, MT202, MT101), while ISO 20022 introduces richer structured fields for improved automation, screening precision, and analytics quality.

MT model: compact, less structured data, higher truncation/repair risk.
ISO 20022: semantic structure, richer remittance, better STP outcomes.
Expand: Why migration matters to executives
Migration is not just technology uplift; it impacts sanctions quality, investigation speed, client reporting depth, and long-term operating leverage.
Module 5

Payment Systems Architecture

Typical architecture chain: Channels → Validation → Screening → Routing → Clearing Interface → Settlement Engine → Reconciliation & Reporting.

  • Channels: portal, API, file gateway, branch system.
  • Processing: batch for volume efficiency, real-time for immediacy.
  • Control overlays: AML, fraud scoring, sanctions and policy gates.

STP performance is a function of upstream data quality and consistent rule governance.

Module 6

Compliance & Regulatory Controls

  • AML: detect suspicious financial behavior patterns.
  • KYC: identify and risk-profile customers/entities.
  • Sanctions screening: prevent restricted party processing.
  • OFAC: globally referenced U.S. sanctions framework.
Expand: Why false positives still matter
False positives increase friction and manual review cost; tuning thresholds and data quality controls are essential for balancing risk and client experience.
Module 7

Exception Handling & Reconciliation

Common exceptions: invalid account details, wrong currency, duplicate files, insufficient funds, sanctions hold.

Repair flow: Queue → Investigate → Correct → Reprocess.

Nostro reconciliation: correspondent statement vs internal position.
GL reconciliation: accounting ledger vs transaction engine.

Nostro and Vostro positions are critical for cross-border liquidity transparency.

Module 8

Modernization Trends

  • Real-time payments and always-on client expectation.
  • API banking and embedded treasury workflows.
  • Open banking enablement with controlled consent patterns.
  • Cloud transformation for scalable and resilient processing.
  • ISO 20022 migration as strategic data modernization.
Expand: Executive success metrics
Track STP uplift, exception rate reduction, manual repair effort, compliance alert precision, reconciliation timeliness, and client SLA adherence.
Presenter note: Close with transformation roadmap: controls first, data quality second, channel modernization third.
